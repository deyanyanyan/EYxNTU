Local LMS Content Generation

A local AI system that turns documents into teaching materials — and, unlike a generator alone, checks its own work at every step before the course moves forward. Pretrained Qwen drafts each artefact from retrieved source evidence; an evaluation layer scores it against that evidence, sends weak output back for bounded revision, and escalates what it cannot repair to an instructor with the reason attached.

4 students  ·  15 weeks  ·  7 September–18 December 2026  ·  Prototype and evaluation  ·  No model training or fine-tuning

What the system will do

  1. 1 · UploadPDF, Word, slides
  2. 2 · Find evidenceSearch source text
  3. 3 · Plan courseObjectives and syllabusEvaluated
  4. 4 · Create lessonsSlides, notes, quizzesEvaluated
  5. 5 · ExportSlides, video, LMS

Where our contribution sits. EY already has a prototype that runs steps 1 to 5 end to end. Producing a course is therefore not our contribution; producing a course whose every step has been checked against its sources and recorded is. Steps 3 and 4 do not advance on the model's say-so — each generated artefact must pass an evaluation gate first.

The gate returns one of three outcomes: pass, revise within a fixed retry budget, or escalate to the instructor with the failing check named. Every outcome is stored, so a finished course carries an auditable record of why each part of it was accepted.

What each gate checks

Grounding

Every factual claim traces to a retrieved passage from the uploaded documents, with a stable reference back to it.

Anchored in code: each claim must quote a span found verbatim in the retrieved text, checked without the model.

Coherence

The artefact matches the approved plan — its stated objective, its position in the syllabus, and the lessons around it.

Anchored in code: objective and lesson references must resolve to the approved plan.

Pedagogy

Objectives carry a stated level, assessments test what the lesson actually taught, and difficulty is declared rather than assumed.

Anchored in code: questions may not leak their answer; every quiz item must map to a taught objective.

How we keep the evaluation honest

A model scoring its own output will tend to approve it. The evaluation layer is only worth building if we can show it is measuring something real, so three safeguards are part of the deliverable rather than added at the end.

1 · A deterministic check under every gate

Each dimension has at least one rule evaluated in ordinary code, not by the model. These cannot be talked around, and they fail loudly. The verbatim-quote check already runs in the quiz generator.

2 · An instructor agreement study

An instructor scores a sample of artefacts blind. We report how closely the agent's scores track theirs. Weak agreement is reported as a finding, not hidden — it tells us which checks are worth keeping.

3 · A like-for-like comparison

Same model, same sources, same rubric across three workflows: single pass, fixed revision, and gated agentic. If the gates do not improve quality or reduce reviewer effort, the report says so.

Support requested from the school

GPU access: evaluating at every step multiplies model calls roughly three- to four-fold over single-pass generation. Measured on a 20-CPU laptop, one structured generation takes 29 seconds and a long evidence-backed call 177 seconds, so a full gated course runs into hours. We request shared school GPU time; an H100 80 GB is the preferred test resource. The minimum viable hardware is one of the things the evaluation will establish.

Setup and guidance: agree GPU booking time, storage, IT support for approved downloads, and — importantly for the agreement study — an instructor reviewer willing to score a sample of generated material. Laptop development continues with the smaller model while access is arranged.

From EY: the output format of the existing prototype, so the evaluation layer can be demonstrated against real generator output rather than only our own.

How we will deliver it

Build one complete course workflow using pretrained models, put an evaluation gate on every step of it, then measure whether those gates improve the result. Phases overlap.

What changed in this revision. Evaluation has moved from a closing activity to the centre of the project, and now runs before the export work rather than after it. Video and LMS packaging are reduced to what a credible demonstration needs, because EY's existing prototype already covers that ground and rebuilding it would spend our remaining weeks on the one part that is not our contribution.

Own parsing, search tools and citation integrity. Own the deterministic grounding check — verbatim span validation and stable references that survive re-upload.

Own lesson generation, the three evaluators and bounded revision, the instructor editing surface that shows why something failed, and reduced-scope export.

DRuntime and evidence

Shared routine: integrate and demo every Friday. Everyone contributes tests, documentation and quality reviews; agree interfaces early so the four parts connect.

What we will hand over

  • A local prototype with an evaluation gate on every generated step, and a sample course produced through it.
  • The evaluation record for that course: every pass, revision and escalation with its reason and supporting evidence.
  • A report comparing single-pass, fixed-revision and gated agentic workflows, plus the instructor agreement results.
  • Code, tests, API and deployment guides, final presentation and demonstration.

How we will judge success

  • Grounding rate — share of generated claims with a verifiable verbatim source span, measured in code.
  • Agreement — how closely agent scores track an instructor's on a blind sample.
  • Reviewer effort — edits required per approved artefact, against the ungated baseline.
  • Cost — model calls, wall-clock time and memory per course, on both CPU and GPU profiles.

Scope: pretrained models; no training or fine-tuning deliverable. Avatars, extra languages and SCORM 2004 remain optional. A negative result — gates that do not improve outcomes — is a reportable finding, not a project failure.

How the system fits together

LangChain connects pretrained Qwen to tools; LangGraph manages the agent workflow inside the Python worker. The evaluation layer sits between generation and storage — nothing reaches an instructor without passing through it. PostgreSQL stores jobs, workflow checkpoints and evaluation records. Models, tools and logs stay local.
